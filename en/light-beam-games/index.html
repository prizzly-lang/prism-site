Games like Lazors and God of Light: 8 light-beam puzzle games

November 24, 2026

If you like bouncing a laser off mirrors until it hits every target, there is more to play than you might think. Here are the ones on phones and PCs. This page is written by A Driven, the company that makes the light puzzle game PRISM. Our game is on the list, and we say plainly where another game is the better fit.

Lazors

Move blocks to bounce a laser, and the level is solved when the beam reaches every target. Over a hundred levels plus a daily puzzle. If you are searching for "games like Lazors", these are the rules you mean. On the App Store, Google Play and Steam.

God of Light

Help a light creature called Shiny send beams to their goal with mirrors, prisms, splitters and filters. A good pick if you care about mood and progression as much as the puzzles. On phones, with a Remastered edition for Switch and Xbox.

Chromatron

A Windows classic by Sean Barrett. Place mirrors and splitters to send colored beams into targets of the same color. All four games were released free in 2007. If you like puzzles that split and mix color, this is where the idea comes from.

Laser Puzzle: Mirror & Light

An Android game where you tap mirrors to rotate them and guide a laser to the goal. Its store listing promises more than 300 handcrafted levels. Worth it if sheer number of levels matters to you.

Prism Out and Prism Riddle (similar names, different games)

Several light puzzles are called Prism. Prism Out is an Android game where you split white light with mirrors, prisms and lenses and hit targets by color. Prism Riddle is a browser game that goes as far as lenses and refraction. Neither is A Driven's PRISM.

Lumear

A daily laser puzzle in the browser, with mirrors, prisms and color filters. Good for a short session without installing anything.

PRISM: Light Puzzle (our game)

Tap a piece and it turns 90 degrees, and the light finds its new path at once. Light every crystal with exactly the color it asks for and the board is solved. Three things set it apart.

  • Every board has exactly one solution. All 300 are proven by exhaustive search, and the tap count shown is the true minimum (how we built them).
  • The chapters follow real optics. Reflection, half-silvered mirrors, dispersion, additive color, filters, spectroscopy and polarization, each opened by a card on the real physics, so teachers use it in class (teacher's guide).
  • You can finish it without seeing color. Every crystal shows its color as a shape too (red triangle, green square, blue circle). No ads, and it works offline.
