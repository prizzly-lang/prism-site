How to teach reflection of light with a game: a 20-minute lesson plan

November 24, 2026 · A Driven Inc.

Students get the idea of reflection quickly once they try it with a mirror, but predicting where reflected light will go takes practice. This lesson uses a puzzle game to make students predict, check and predict again many times in a few minutes. The game is chapter 1 of PRISM, a light puzzle by A Driven, which opens in a web browser with nothing to install and no sign-up.

At a glance

ItemDetails
ForAges 9 to 12 (KS2 Light, NGSS 4-PS4-2); also works as an opener at ages 11 to 14 (KS3 Light waves, MS-PS4-2)
Time20 minutes
GoalStudents know that light travels in straight lines and changes direction at a mirror, and can predict which way a reflected beam will go from the way a mirror faces
You needPer group: one hand mirror, one flashlight (torch) and one tablet or computer with a browser; printed worksheets

Lesson flow

TimeStudentsTeacher
0 to 5 minMirror demo: lay a mirror flat, shine a flashlight on it and find the bright spot on the ceiling. Tilt the flashlight, then tilt the mirrorAsk before each step: "Why is there a spot?" "If we tilt the mirror, where will the spot go?"
5 to 13 minPredict with the game: in pairs, play PRISM chapter 1. Before one partner taps a mirror, the other says "the beam will turn up" or "down"Have pairs count correct predictions. If a pair is stuck, only say: "Follow the beam with your finger. Where does it stop now?"
13 to 18 minDraw it: on the worksheet, trace the path of light on one board in pencil. Older students draw the normal and mark the angles of incidence and reflectionShow two or three drawings to the class and agree together: "Light goes straight, and only turns at a mirror."
18 to 20 minCheck: write an answer to one exit questionPut one of the exit questions below on the board

Before the lesson

  • Open the PRISM browser demo. It has all 14 boards of chapter 1, easiest first. The first four or five are plenty for this lesson.
  • Print the worksheet Steering Light with Mirrors (two A4 pages plus an answer key). Colors are also marked with shapes, so it works on a black and white printer.
  • For background, the reflection of light concept page has the mirror experiment, the law of reflection and check questions.

Safety: agree beforehand that nobody shines a flashlight, or light bounced off a mirror, into anyone's eyes. Do not use cracked mirrors.

Why use a game?

The mirror demo shows that reflection happens, but once you have seen it, it is done. In the game, every tap changes the beam's path at once, so students can predict and check dozens of times in a few minutes. Each board has exactly one solution, so nothing is solved by luck, and when a prediction is wrong the screen shows exactly where the beam went instead.

The game is simpler than real light. Beams only travel up, down, left or right, and a mirror has only two positions, "/" and "\", so light always turns through exactly 90 degrees. End the lesson by pointing out that a real mirror reflects light arriving at any angle, with the angle of reflection equal to the angle of incidence.

Common misconceptions and what to say

  • "Light goes into the mirror." Cover the mirror with a hand and the spot on the ceiling disappears. The light bounces off the surface.
  • "Something comes out of our eyes so we can see." We see objects because light reflected from them enters our eyes. Turn off the lights and we cannot see them.
  • (Older students) "Measure the angles from the mirror." Both angles are measured from the normal. Having students draw the normal first on the worksheet helps.

Exit questions

  1. If you shine a flashlight straight at a mirror, at right angles, where does the reflected light go? (Answer: straight back the way it came.)
  2. In the game, a beam heading right meets a "/" mirror. Which way does it go? (Answer: up.)
  3. (Older students) The angle of incidence is 40 degrees. What is the angle of reflection? (Answer: 40 degrees.)

Extension

  • Fast finishers: the later boards of chapter 1, or the worksheet Light Around Corners (how a periscope works).
  • Next lessons: half-silvered mirrors and prisms follow in the same order as the game. See the teacher's guide to all seven chapters.
