Splitting Light in Two: a printable beam splitter worksheet

A worksheet built from four early boards of chapter 2, where beam splitters first appear. It has two A4 pages (front and back) for students and a one-page answer key for teachers. On the front, students follow the light as a beam splitter divides it in two and send it to every crystal. On the back, they think about the two beams, the beam that stays straight when you turn the splitter, beams that cross, and branches that never reach a crystal.

It works on a black and white printer: every color of light is also marked with a shape (red , green , blue ).

Beam splitters are an extension topic beyond the standard curriculum. The reflection behind them is taught in upper elementary science. It is easier after the “Steering Light with Mirrors” worksheet.

Where the two beams go

How to play

  1. Light leaves the source (the square box) in the direction of its small arrow. It keeps going straight until it meets something.
  2. When light reaches a beam splitter (the piece drawn with two lines), it becomes two beams. One carries on straight the way it came, and one bends 90 degrees like it would off a mirror. Both beams have the same color.
  3. Each tap turns a mirror or a beam splitter 90 degrees, so it swaps between a “/” shape and a “\” shape. Crystals and sources never move. The board is solved when every crystal (hexagon) receives light of the color drawn inside it.

Circle the pieces you would turn, then draw the path of the light from the source to the crystals in pencil. When the light splits, draw both beams.

Ways of thinking about splitting light

1One beam becomes two

Light that reaches a beam splitter does not disappear and does not turn into just one beam. It becomes two: one that goes straight and one that bends. Both are the color of the light that came in.

On board 1, when the blue light reaches the beam splitter in D3, which way does each of the two beams go?

2Turning changes only the bending beam

When you turn a beam splitter, the beam that bends goes the other way. The beam that goes straight keeps going straight whichever way the splitter is turned. On these boards you often do not need to turn the beam splitter at all.

On board 3, which crystal is lit before you turn anything? On the solved board, which of the two beams from the beam splitter lights that crystal?

3When split beams cross

When two split beams meet in a cell, they do not block each other and each carries on as it was. Light bends or splits only in a cell that holds a mirror or a beam splitter.

On the solved board 2, the two branches from the splitter meet again in one cell. Which cell is it, and what fills it?

What would happen to the two beams if a mirror stood in that cell?

4Not every branch has to reach a crystal

Each split adds a branch. Not every branch has to end on a crystal. One may simply leave the board.

On board 4 the light meets a beam splitter twice. On the solved board, how many beams does the light end up as? How many of them reach a crystal?

Which beam splitter does the beam that leaves the board split off from, and which cells does it pass through?
