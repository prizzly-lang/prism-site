Mirror Board Challenge: a printable worksheet on reflection

A worksheet built from four later boards of chapter 1, each with three or four mirrors to turn. It has two A4 pages (front and back) for students and a one-page answer key for teachers. On the front, students find which mirrors to turn and send the light to the crystals. On the back, they think about the number of turns, tracing a beam backwards, beams that cross, and light stopped by another source.

It works on a black and white printer: every color of light is also marked with a shape (red , green ).

For ages about 9 to 12, in a unit on reflection. Connects to NGSS 4-PS4-2 (US) and KS2 Light, Year 6 (England). It is easier after the “Steering Light with Mirrors” and “Light Around Corners” worksheets.

How to play

  1. Light leaves the source (the square box) in the direction of its small arrow. It keeps going straight until it meets something.
  2. Each tap turns a mirror 90 degrees, so it swaps between a “/” shape and a “\” shape. Crystals and sources never move.
  3. The board is solved when every crystal (hexagon) receives light of the color drawn inside it.

Circle the mirrors you would turn, then draw the path of the light from the source to the crystal in pencil.

Ways of thinking about big boards

1Turn a mirror at most once

A mirror has only two shapes, “/” and “\”. One turn gives the other shape, and a second turn brings it back. So you never need to turn a mirror twice, and the fewest turns is the number of mirrors that start out the wrong way.

On board 1, does any mirror start out the right way? How many turns does it take in all?

2Tracing back from the crystal

On these boards the path of the light is the same read backwards. Start at the crystal and go back the way the light came, and you reach the source. When the path is tangled, going backwards from the crystal can be easier than going forwards from the source.

On the solved board 3, start at the crystal in D1 and go down, back along the beam. List the mirrors you meet, in order, by cell name. What do you reach at the end?

3When beams cross

When two beams meet in a cell, they do not collide or block each other. They carry on as they were. Light only bends in a cell that holds a mirror.

On the solved board 2, in which cell do the two beams cross? What is in that cell?

What would have changed if one of the two beams had met a mirror in C2?

4Light stopped by another source Extension

In this game, light that reaches another light source ends there. Having a source does not mean its light is used to light a crystal.

On board 4, which cells does the light from the source in E1 pass through, and where does it end?

On board 4, which source does the light that lights the crystal come from? How many mirrors does it meet?
